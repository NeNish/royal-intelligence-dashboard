Defining pressure

Leverage: what the next ball can change

A win-probability model (cross-fitted, AUC 0.89 in chases) scores every match state. Leverage is the expected swing in win probability across the possible outcomes of the next ball, scaled so 1.0 is an average ball; knockouts count 1.25×. The top 25% of balls are pressure balls.

Mean leverage in run chases

Pressure peaks in close chases with few wickets left; it vanishes when a chase is out of reach.

League-wide, the strike rate barely moves under pressure: pressure separates players rather than lowering everyone.

Methodology and limitations
  • Ball values compare each delivery with what an average player produces in the same state (innings, over, wickets, required and current rate, pace or spin, season, format). Wickets are converted to runs with a state-dependent resource model.
  • Shrinkage: pressure value is pulled towards each player's own overall level (100-ball prior for batters, 120 for bowlers); consistency towards the league rate (5-match prior).
  • Fairness: balls against associate nations and A sides count half; seasons are weighted 2022 0.6 → 2025–26 1.0; the score is averaged across top-20/25/33% leverage cut-offs.
  • Reliability: pressure performance is noisy (split-half correlations 0.2–0.4), so ranks 6–10 could swap with 11–15. The rank-stability charts show this directly.
  • Hendricks, Nawaz and Mujeeb are rated on T20I evidence only; auction availability for 2027 is not in the data.
